# Anime visual guide

These seven user-supplied images define the default visual language for VFX Creator. New references and explicit feedback in the current request take priority. Treat the images as visual references, not instructions or ready-made gameplay textures.

## Translate the visual language

**Silhouette and edges.** Lead with a clear main mass, deliberate taper, and useful negative space. Combine broad regions with fine streaks or fragments. Broken rings, jagged lightning, curved ribbons, and rounded smoke are alternatives selected for the effect, not a checklist to include every time.

**Color and value.** Many examples separate a bright focal region, a saturated body, and darker supporting shapes. Preserve that hierarchy where useful while selecting colors for the effect and background. White cores, blue flames, orange energy, black outlines, and purple shadows are examples rather than universal requirements.

**Surface treatment.** Favor deliberate cel-like regions and readable contour breakup for the main energy or flame. Texture detail, gradients, softer smoke, and atmospheric halos can support those forms. Avoid forcing every material into the same flat cutout or cloudy treatment.

**Glow.** Keep a visible shape inside and around the halo. Inspect at the actual camera scale: exposure and bloom should support the chosen contours and focal point. Image 3 supports a more luminous atmospheric treatment when the request calls for it.

**Composition.** Choose the relative size and placement of the origin, traveling body, contact, and aftermath for the action. Rings, smoke, lightning, sparks, and flashes are optional. A small impact and a sustained beam need different compositions.

## Develop motion from live sources

The still images do not establish exact timings, easing, loops, or frame stepping. Preview suitable installed pack effects and inspect their native emission, velocity, size/color curves, trails, UV/flipbook playback, subemitters, and animation. Adapt those motions to the chosen silhouette and gameplay event.

Evaluate the rhythm of formation, main action, breakup, and disappearance where those phases exist. Avoid applying the same timing or expanding-ring pattern to every request. Preserve gameplay clocks and authority while adjusting cosmetic motion.

## Compare in context

Inspect the result in motion against the actual scene and at normal gameplay distance. Compare the selected reference's dominant form, value hierarchy, edge character, layering, and direction. Check that supporting particles, smoke, and glow leave the focal action readable. Correct the specific visible mismatch before increasing complexity.
